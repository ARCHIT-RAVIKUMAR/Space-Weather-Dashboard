Why it matters

Satellites. Energetic particles from flares and coronal mass ejections (CMEs) can upset onboard electronics. During storms, the upper atmosphere heats and expands, increasing drag on low-orbit satellites. In February 2022, SpaceX lost around 38 newly launched Starlink satellites to a minor storm for exactly this reason.

Radio & GPS. X-ray bursts ionise the sunlit side of the ionosphere and can black out HF radio used by aviation and ships. Disturbed ionosphere also degrades GNSS accuracy, which affects surveying, farming and aircraft approach systems.

Power grids. Rapidly changing magnetic fields induce currents in long conductors. Strong storms can overload transformers, as in the 1989 Quebec blackout, which left millions without power for about nine hours.

Astronauts. Outside Earth's magnetic shield, crews face higher radiation doses during solar particle events. Mission planners watch the S-scale and may move crews to better-shielded areas of the spacecraft. Aircrew on polar routes can be rerouted too.
